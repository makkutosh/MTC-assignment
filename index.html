9 in 10

New York subway riders

More than two million people commute by public transport in the New York area. Nine in ten of the city’s transit users are exposed to noise above the recommended 70 dB, enough to risk irreversible hearing loss.

78+ dB

Ho Chi Minh City, by bicycle

A study followed cyclists across more than 1,000 km of Vietnam’s biggest city. Their exposure went above 78 dB, a level that could damage hearing.

60 dB

Your body hears it

Background noise at 60 dB is enough to raise your heart rate and blood pressure and to cost you sleep and concentration.

60 dB: heart rate and focus start to suffer. 70 dB: the recommended limit. 78 dB: hearing could be damaged. The white needle follows the live counter.

Over half of the residents of large European cities live where noise harms their health. Lower-income residents in poor-quality housing are more exposed to traffic noise.

+8%

Toronto: noise reaches the heart

Two studies found that exposure to traffic noise raised the risk of heart attacks and congestive heart failure.

Earlier

Bogotá’s sparrows start singing sooner

Researchers found that rufous-collared sparrows begin their dawn chorus earlier, in anticipation of rush hour. The city has changed the rhythm of the birds.

Illustration of the finding, not measured times.

Greener

Trees turn the volume down

Plant-filled rooftops, tree belts, shrubs and green walls absorb and diffuse noise. They also attract urban wildlife, which brings natural sounds back.

Public parks offer acoustic relief: rustling leaves and chirping birds reduce stress. UNEP says planners should count these health benefits.
